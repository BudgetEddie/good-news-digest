How we pick stories

The Good News Website runs by itself. Every few hours it reads the latest stories from trusted news sources and publishes only the ones that are genuinely good news.

How a story gets in

  1. New stories from the last three days are collected, and duplicates are removed.
  2. An AI model reads each headline and the publisher's own short description, and decides whether it is genuinely good news: a discovery, a recovery, a success, an act of kindness, or real progress.
  3. It rates each story from 1 to 5. Only stories rated or higher appear here.
  4. It writes a short summary using only what the publisher wrote.

What we leave out

  • Politics, including good news about politicians, parties and elections.
  • Stories that are mainly about tragedy, crime, conflict or scandal, even if they end on a hopeful note.
  • Earnings, funding rounds and product launches, unless they clearly help people.
  • Sports results, celebrity news, shopping deals and sponsored content.

Mistakes happen

No person checks each story before it goes up. Now and then the AI may let in a story that isn't really good news, or get a detail wrong in a summary. Every card links to the original article, so please read it there before sharing.

Credit where it's due

Headlines and articles belong to their publishers. We show the headline, a short summary and a link, and send you to the publisher to read the full story.
